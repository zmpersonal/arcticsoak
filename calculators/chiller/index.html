What size chiller does a cold plunge need?

Cooling 100 gallons from 80°F to 50°F in eight hours requires 3,128 BTU/hr for the water alone. Real equipment must also overcome ambient heat gain, plumbing and pump heat.

Water-only cooling rate
3,128

Planning capacity with 35% headroom: 4,222 BTU/hr.

Water-only load: 0.92 kW thermal.

Do not select equipment from “horsepower” alone. Compare the manufacturer's delivered BTU/hr at your expected ambient and target temperatures.

The sizing formula

required BTU/hr = gallons × 8.34 lb/gal × (start°F − target°F) ÷ cooldown hours

This first calculates the heat that must leave the water. Then add headroom for heat entering through the tub, open lid, plumbing, pump and sun. Hotter ambient conditions may also reduce the chiller's delivered capacity.

Worked example: 100 × 8.34 × (80 − 50) ÷ 8 = 3,128 BTU/hr. Adding 35% planning headroom gives 4,222 BTU/hr. Verify the selected chiller can deliver that output under the actual operating conditions.

Why horsepower is not enough

“1/2 HP” or “1 HP” can describe compressor motor class, not a guaranteed cooling output. The useful comparison is delivered BTU/hr at a stated ambient temperature, water temperature and flow rate. A unit that performs adequately in a garage may struggle outdoors in direct summer sun.

Hold load versus cooldown load

Cooldown sizing asks how quickly you can remove a one-time body of heat. Hold load asks how much heat continuously enters after the target is reached. Choose a chiller that can handle both, with room for filter loading and hot-weather performance loss.
